## Nyquist Analysis

The signal frequency is **10 Hz**. The theoretical Nyquist threshold is

\[
F_{s,\min} = 2 f_{\max} = 2 \times 10 = \boxed{20\ \text{Hz}}.
\]

Sampling period is **\(T_s = 1/F_s\)**. Frequencies **20, 25, 50 and 100 Hz** meet or exceed the numerical Nyquist threshold. **15 Hz** does not. In practice, the equality case **20 Hz is not a robust choice**: for the zero-phase sine wave in the script, every sample falls at a zero crossing, yielding all zeros. The theoretical reconstruction condition for a general bandlimited signal is usually stated with sampling strictly above twice the highest signal frequency, together with suitable anti-alias filtering.

## Aliasing Discussion (maximum 200 words)

At **15 Hz**, sampling the 10 Hz wave violates the Nyquist requirement. The discrete samples can be interpreted as a lower-frequency 5 Hz signal because the continuous oscillation changes too quickly relative to the sample rate. At **20 Hz**, the sampling rate lies on the Nyquist boundary; the chosen zero-phase sine produces samples at its zero crossings, so this is not reliable despite meeting the numerical threshold. At **25, 50 and 100 Hz**, the 10 Hz tone is below the Nyquist limit, so that tone does not alias in ideal sampling. The plots should show increasingly dense sampling as the rate rises. For a vibration sensor known to contain useful content only up to 10 Hz, I would choose **100 Hz** for this experiment, provided an appropriate analog anti-alias filter rejects higher-frequency components before sampling. This gives ten samples per cycle and more flexibility for analysis while remaining a modest data rate. Real machines may contain higher-frequency vibrations, so the actual sampling rate must be based on the highest useful frequency and the sensor's full measured spectrum.

## Engineering Recommendation

For the **specified 10 Hz signal**, choose **Fs = 100 Hz** (10 samples per cycle; 100 samples per second per channel) rather than operating at the 20 Hz limit. This is a design recommendation for the assignment signal, **not a universal vibration-monitoring sample rate**. Assess any higher-frequency machine faults, sensor bandwidth, storage, computational constraints and required anti-alias filter before selecting a real deployment rate.
